Porcelain, sandstone or concrete: choosing patio paving

What each type of patio slab is like to live with, and why the base matters more than the slab.

Paving

The slab you pick decides how a patio looks. The base underneath decides how long it stays looking that way. Here is a plain guide to both.

Porcelain

Porcelain is hard, even in colour and does not soak up water, so it resists staining and green growth better than anything else. It suits modern gardens and needs very little upkeep.

  • Watch out for: it costs more and must be laid on a full mortar bed with a bonding primer.

Natural stone

Sandstone and limestone have natural variation that suits most houses. Every slab is slightly different, which gives a softer, more traditional look.

  • Watch out for: stone is porous, so it will weather and pick up algae sooner. A yearly wash keeps it right.

Concrete slabs

Concrete paving is the budget option and comes in a wide range of finishes. Good-quality concrete slabs laid well will last for many years.

  • Watch out for: colour can fade over time in strong sun and weather.

The part you do not see

Whatever slab you choose, it needs a dug-out, compacted hardcore base, a slight fall so rain runs away from the house, and a finished level that sits below the damp-proof course. Skipping any of these is how patios end up sinking, rocking or holding puddles.
