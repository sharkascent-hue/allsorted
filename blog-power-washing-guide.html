Power washing: what it can and cannot fix

Where power washing works well, where it can do damage, and how to keep surfaces clean for longer.

Exterior Cleaning

Power washing is one of the quickest ways to make a property look cared for again. Used on the wrong surface or at the wrong pressure, it can also cause damage. Here is what to know.

Where it works well

  • Concrete and block paving driveways
  • Patios and garden paths that have turned green and slippery
  • Garden walls and hard standing

Where care is needed

  • Render and pointing: too much pressure can blow out mortar and mark render.
  • Timber decking and fences: high pressure raises the grain and leaves stripes.
  • Old tarmac: loose stone can be lifted out.

On these surfaces a wider fan, more distance and lower pressure do the job without harm.

After the clean

Block paving should have its joints re-sanded once dry, as washing removes the sand that locks the blocks together. Timber is best treated or stained soon after cleaning, while it is bare and clean.

Keeping it clean

Moss and algae come back fastest in shade and where water sits. A light clean once a year is easier on the surface than a heavy clean every five.
