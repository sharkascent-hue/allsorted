Timber, composite or PVC: which fence is right for your garden?

The three main fence materials compared on looks, upkeep, lifespan and cost.

Fencing

Most Dublin gardens end up with one of three fence types. None is "the best" — they suit different budgets and different levels of patience for upkeep. Here is how they compare.

Timber

Timber is the classic choice and usually the lowest cost up front. Pressure-treated panels on concrete posts with a concrete gravel board underneath will give you the longest life, because the timber never sits in wet soil.

  • Good for: a natural look, tighter budgets, matching existing fences.
  • Watch out for: it needs a coat of treatment or stain every couple of years to stay looking well.

Composite

Composite boards are made from a mix of wood fibre and recycled plastic. They slot between posts as horizontal slats and come in greys, greens and wood-effect tones. They do not rot, warp or need painting.

  • Good for: a modern look and almost no maintenance.
  • Watch out for: higher cost up front than timber.

PVC

PVC fencing gives a clean, bright finish in white, cream or beige, often with a lattice top. It will not rot and washes down with a hose.

  • Good for: a tidy, uniform look and easy cleaning.
  • Watch out for: fewer colour choices, and lighter colours show dirt sooner.

Still not sure?

The quickest way to decide is to see the materials side by side. We bring samples on every site visit, so you can hold them up against your own house and garden before choosing.
